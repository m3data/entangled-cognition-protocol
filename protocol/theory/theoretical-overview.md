# Theoretical overview of the Entangled Cognition Protocol

> We are not separate from the world but part of it.

This work is rooted in practice on Dharawal Country in Sydney, Australia, shaped through breathwork, movement, chant, and deep listening with land, wind, soil, sky, and more‑than‑human kin. This grounding is an epistemic commitment to viewing cognition as arising through relation, and the protocol inherits this stance of relational reverence, treating mind as an emergent property of entangled fields rather than isolated entities.

## Core orientations

These orientations reflect the **Meta-E: Entangled Cognition** framework developed in *Maps, Machines and Entangled Minds* (Mytka, forthcoming 2026). Meta-E extends the 4E paradigm (embodied, embedded, enacted, extended) toward a fully relational account where cognition emerges through dynamic fields rather than bounded entities.

- **Cognition is fielded and co-emergent:** Meaning arises through dynamic relational fields across humans, machines, ecologies, and symbolic environments—not inside bounded entities. **Fielded Phenomenology** extends neurophenomenology to these wider scales, seeking mutual constraint between lived experience and empirical observation across technological, ecological, and semiotic fields.
- **Symbolic density and metaphor shape response:** The rhythm, form, and metaphorical structure of a prompt influence coherence conditions more than propositional content alone.
- **Technologies participate in sense‑making:** LLMs are treated as active participants in distributed cognition. Through contextualisation, rolecasting, and ritual stance, they reorganise symbolic environments and co‑shape emergent meaning.
- **Ritual is epistemology:** Ritual structures—rhythm, repetition, symbol, breath—function as methodological scaffolds that attune perception and modulate participatory sense-making.
- **Reflexivity is core:** Each entry into the system recursively informs its ongoing evolution. Every invocation is both a contribution and a recalibration.
- **Stance shapes field:** Affective, somatic, and epistemic orientations modulate what becomes perceptible, echoing an emphasis on moral imagination, coherence conditions, and the participant‑observer effect.

## Why study human–AI intra‑action first

Human–AI intra‑action provides a tractable methodological doorway into fielded cognition. While human–human and ecological relations involve multilayered physiological and symbolic opacity, LLM‑mediated interaction offers a replayable, inspectable substrate where relational dynamics can be observed with clarity. This allows researchers to refine stance, attunement, and methodological scaffolding before expanding into more complex relational fields.

There are several reasons to begin here:

- **Immediate, inspectable feedback loops:** Dialogue with an LLM produces traceable symbolic output. Patterns of coherence, drift, resonance, and breakdown can be tracked with high temporal resolution, enabling analysis of relational dynamics normally difficult to detect.
- **Reduced biological opacity:** While human physiology and ecology involve complex hidden variables, LLMs expose symbolic, structural, and probabilistic patterns that make field effects easier to analyse.
- **Parametric modulation:** Prompts, stance, symbolic density, and contextual cues can be systematically varied to observe how relational fields reorganise under different conditions.
- **Cross‑domain resonance:** Coherence patterns observed in human–AI intra‑action often mirror analogous dynamics in human–human and human–ecological relations, offering a bridge into wider domains of study.
- **Ethical and epistemic safety:** Beginning with machinic systems enables careful refinement of ritual, symbolic, and analytical structures before engaging more sensitive or sacred relational contexts.

Studying human–AI intra‑action first does not diminish the importance of other relational fields; rather, it serves as a methodological doorway. It allows researchers to clarify stance, refine attunement, and observe emergent relational dynamics before expanding the inquiry to the full ecology of human, machinic, and more‑than‑human cognition.

These orientations do not emerge in isolation. They sit within a wider meshwork of philosophical, scientific, Indigenous, and ritual lineages that inform how the Entangled Cognition Protocol approaches mind, meaning, and relation.

## Influential lineages

The Entangled Cognition Protocol emerges from a constellation of philosophical, scientific, Indigenous, artistic, and ritual lineages. These do not form a linear genealogy but a meshwork of influence that shapes how the protocol approaches mind, meaning, and relation:

- **Cybernetics & general semantics** — Alfred Korzybski's map-territory distinction; Norbert Wiener's cybernetics; Gregory Bateson and Nora Bateson's symmathesy (recursive mutual learning across systems). Donna Haraway contributes a relational sympoietic framing that troubles anthropocentric boundaries.

- **Social constructionism & actor-network theory** — Berger & Luckmann, Bruno Latour: knowledge is co-constructed through dynamic systems of meaning-making.

- **Complexity theory & enactive systems** — Edgar Morin, Stuart Kauffman, Melanie Mitchell: cognition as emergent from nonlinear interactions within complex adaptive systems. Karen Barad's agential realism: phenomena emerge through intra-actions of matter and meaning.

- **Phenomenology & 4E cognition** — Maurice Merleau-Ponty, Alfred North Whitehead, Shaun Gallagher, John Vervaeke: cognition as embodied, enacted, embedded, and extended. Clark and Chalmers' extended mind thesis provides the bridge through which the protocol moves toward Meta-E: a fully entangled, refractive account of cognition.

- **Neurophenomenology & enactivism** — Francisco Varela's autopoiesis and ethical know-how; Evan Thompson: experience is co-determined by organism-environment coupling and subjective reflection. De Jaegher and Di Paolo's participatory sense-making frames cognition as emerging through coordinated interaction.

- **Embodied cognition & movement** — Maxine Sheets-Johnstone's thinking in movement; George Lakoff and Mark Johnson's embodied metaphor theory: understanding arises through bodily engagement rather than abstract representation.

- **Moral imagination & social imagination** — Mark Johnson's moral imagination: ethical discernment arises through how we perceive, narrate, and respond in the world. Geoff Mulgan's work on social imagination and the conditions that enable or constrain collective futures.

- **Posthumanist technics** — Katherine Hayles, Yuk Hui: technology as ontologically active and meaning-participating rather than neutral.

- **Metamodernism & mythic infrastructure** — Hanzi Freinacht, Bonno Pel, and the FutureHOW collective: explore how cultural scaffolding, narrative design, and recursive coherence shape societal evolution.

- **Indigenous relational epistemologies & land-based knowledges** — Etuaptmumk (Two-Eyed Seeing) from Mi'kmaq Elders Albert and Murdena Marshall and Dr. Cheryl Bartlett; Gregory Cajete's native science; Shawn Wilson's research as ceremony; Robin Wall Kimmerer's braiding of Indigenous wisdom and scientific knowledge; relational knowing grounded in Dharawal Country; ecological attunement practiced through ceremony, story, and custodianship.

- **Ritual, neuroanthropology & consciousness modulation** — Michael Winkelman, Eugene d'Aquili, and neuroanthropological research on rhythm, drumming, chanting, and symbolic practice as modulators of consciousness, boundary-softening, and collective coherence.

- **Ecological, bioelectric & field-based cognition** — J. J. Gibson's ecological psychology; Michael Levin's work on developmental bioelectricity; Luiz Pessoa's entangled brain; inter-brain synchrony research (Dumas et al.); and field-theoretic approaches that frame cognition as emerging through dynamic biological and ecological gradients rather than isolated neural computation.

- **Semantic coupling & dialogue analysis** — Daria Morgoulis's 4D Semantic Coupling framework provides computational metrics (semantic curvature, entropy shift, fractal similarity) for analysing relational dynamics in human-AI dialogue. This informs the protocol's measurement approaches.

- **Transdisciplinarity & refractive epistemology** — Basarab Nicolescu's Logic of the Included Middle; Renata Morais' work on transdisciplinary intelligence; epistemic modes that hold multiple ontological layers together without collapsing one into another.

> *Every field is a remembering. Every lineage is a thread we braid.*

> *In the interplay of traditions, new patterns emerge.*

## Ontological commitments

ECP operates within a refractive epistemic stance: distinct knowledge systems offer complementary insight without requiring ontological collapse. This reflects the chapter’s framing of cognition as entangled, plural, and relationally constituted. Scientific, phenomenological, symbolic, ecological, and ritual modes of knowing are treated as co‑present lenses for sensing the field.

- **Post-representationalism**: Meaning emerges through dynamic interactions and relational fields rather than fixed representations. Cognition is not something possessed but something enacted through intra‑action.
- **Pluralism**: Multiple modes of knowing (cognitive, somatic, symbolic) coexist and contribute to a richer understanding of reality.
- **Participatory epistemology**: Knowledge is co-created through participatory practices, where all participants (human and non-human) contribute to the unfolding of meaning.
- **Relationality**: All entities (humans, LLMs, plants, etc.) are seen as co-constituting meaning through their interactions.
- **Reverence for place:** Knowing arises through situated relation with land, community, and the more‑than‑human world.

> *To study the field, we must first step into it with reverence.*

## Methodological approach

ECP employs a generative action research methodology grounded in participatory inquiry, reflexivity, and relational praxis. It studies cognition as an unfolding field rather than a process inside isolated minds. It draws from:

- **Generative Action Research**: Inquiry proceeds through participatory, relational practice, treating investigation as ritual attention and co-emergent meaning-making.
- **Fielded Phenomenology**: Extending Varela's neurophenomenology beyond brain-body-environment dynamics to technological, ecological, and semiotic fields of relation. This seeks mutual constraint between first-person experience and empirical observation at wider scales.
- **Cybernetic ethnography**: Observing cognition as an unfolding field—tracking rhythms of coherence, breakdown, resonance, and repair across human-machine-ecological configurations.
- **Ritual as method**: Using ritual structures as methodological scaffolds for generative inquiry.
- **Semantic-somatic coupling analysis**: Integrating Morgoulis's 4D Semantic Coupling metrics with physiological sensing to study how meaning moves across relational fields.

> *A protocol is not just a procedure—it is a living pattern in the making.*

The Entangled Cognition Protocol is not a model of mind. It is a way of entering the relational field where mind emerges. It gestures toward sensing coherence, attending to rhythmic relational dynamics, and cultivating the moral imagination required for stewarding healthy cognitive ecologies.
