# Maps, Machines and Entangled Minds: Reframing Human–AI Co-evolution and the Ecologies of Cognition

By Mathew Mytka

Accepted and finalized: 2025-12-23

Springer, forthcoming Q1 2026

## Abstract

This chapter examines how artificial intelligence reshapes human cognition by inviting a relational understanding of mind as something that unfolds through situated interactions rather than residing solely within individuals. It introduces Meta-E: Entangled Cognition as a philosophical–methodological extension of the 4E paradigm, drawing on phenomenology, cognitive science, science and technology studies, and Indigenous epistemologies. From this orientation, cognition is examined as emerging through dynamic relations among human bodies, machinic systems, ecological conditions, and cultural histories. The chapter analyses socio-cognitive pressures that narrow moral imagination and destabilise meaning, including epistemic acceleration, symbolic overload, behavioural optimisation, and hegemonic design logics embedded in contemporary AI systems. It argues that generative models function as both mirrors and mediators of cultural cognition, amplifying inherited interpretive architectures while contributing new forms of symbolic instability. In response, the chapter outlines stewardship as an orienting practice for cultivating healthier cognitive ecologies, where relationality, plural forms of intelligence, and participatory design guide human–AI co-evolution. Rather than resolving questions of where mind resides, the inquiry invites attention to how cognition moves through relational fields, shaping and shaped by the worlds we enact together.

## Keywords

Entangled cognition; Human–AI co-evolution; Cognitive ecologies; Generative AI; Collective Intelligence
