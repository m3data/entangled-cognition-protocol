# Kaleidescopes and Two-Eyed Seeing

Now, two-eyed seeing is an interesting reference here, and even the metaphorical way of understanding it, that, through one eye you might lean towards the qualitative, relational ways of knowing much more familiar to indigenous epistemologies and the very languages that they use to interface with reality, and another eye that's the analytical, that's the quantitative, that's creating these maps to understand the territory as a constant source of collapsing uncertainty. And a kaleidoscope might actually be a much more helpful way of understanding this as a metaphor than binoculars. Yes, Ian McGilchrist's work, The Master and the Emissary, and the research on hemispheric dominance and neglect, and that is possibly quite an important frame here, where our left and right hemispheres of our nervous system, including our brain, but not just our brain, it also includes the importance of the gut-mind axis, the influence of the foods we eat on the production of things like serotonin in my body as a neurochemical constituent of cognition itself, which is again a reference to the entanglements of cognition itself. But the kaleidoscope is a much more dynamic way of understanding this isnt it? Where through one eye you might be seeing patterns of relationships, colors, shapes, and through another eye you might be seeing the analytical breakdown of those patterns into their constituent parts. And as you turn the kaleidoscope, the patterns shift and change, revealing new configurations and relationships that were not apparent before. This dynamic interplay between the two ways of seeing allows for a more holistic understanding of reality, like where both the qualitative and quantitative aspects are integrated into a cohesive whole. It also emphasizes the importance of perspective and context in shaping our understanding of the world around us.
